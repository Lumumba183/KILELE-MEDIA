Kericho County Assembly Launches Probe into Alleged Ksh.80 Million Fraud Involving Ghost Contractors

22 Jul 2025·2 min read·Kilele News Desk

The Kericho County Assembly has initiated a comprehensive investigation into allegations of a massive fraud involving Ksh.80 million, where public funds were reportedly funneled to non-existent contractors and suppliers. This development has sparked widespread concern over corruption and mismanagement within the county’s financial systems.

The probe, announced by the assembly, aims to uncover the details of how these funds were allegedly misappropriated, with preliminary findings suggesting that the money was paid to ghost entities, a practice that has become a recurring issue in Kenyan public finance management. The investigation is being led by an ad-hoc committee chaired by Vincent Korir, with key figures including Deputy Speaker Cheruiyot Bett and Majority Leader Philip Rono playing pivotal roles in the oversight process.

This scandal comes at a time when Kenya is facing heightened scrutiny over public fund mismanagement, as seen in recent high-profile cases such as the Rex Masai murder inquest, which also involved allegations against public officials. The Kericho case underscores a troubling pattern of financial indiscipline that has long plagued the country’s public sector, leading to significant losses and eroding public trust.

According to sources within the assembly, the alleged fraud involved payments that were not backed by any tangible services or goods, pointing to a sophisticated scheme that may have involved collusion at various levels of the county government. The assembly’s move to launch this probe is seen as a critical step towards accountability, with officials vowing to ensure that those responsible are held to account.

The issue of “ghost workers” and contractors is not new to Kenya. A 2014 audit revealed that over 12,500 ghost names were on the national payroll, costing the government millions each month. Similarly, the Kericho case highlights the persistent challenge of financial transparency and the need for robust oversight mechanisms to prevent such abuses.

Citizens and watchdog groups have welcomed the investigation but are calling for swift action and transparency in the process. “This is a clear violation of public trust, and we expect the assembly to follow through with decisive measures,” said a local activist, emphasizing the importance of restoring faith in governance.

The Kericho County Assembly’s probe is part of a broader national conversation on corruption, spurred by recent reports and international indices like Transparency International’s Corruption Perceptions Index, which continue to rank Kenya poorly. The outcome of this investigation could set a precedent for how similar cases are handled across the country, potentially influencing future reforms in public financial management.
